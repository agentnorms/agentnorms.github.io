AI evaluation and decision science

AgentNorm is an independent research group dedicated to advancing AI evaluation and decision science for public benefit. We study how AI systems are evaluated in reasoning and research, including the limits of verification, methods for combining language-model judgments, and the assumptions behind deployment-safety claims.

Our mission is to make rigorous AI research useful and accessible. Through open papers, shared research tools, and student mentorship, we aim to help researchers and communities assess the evidence behind AI systems.

Research

We conduct open research for the public interest: making evaluation assumptions explicit, testing claims about AI systems, and sharing methods and artifacts that others can inspect and reproduce.

Verification and generalization

We study when verified outputs can support claims about a model’s performance, and how gaps in task coverage affect those claims.

Language-model evaluation

Workshop papers

↑ Contents

Accepted ICML 2026 workshop posters. These are non-archival workshop contributions.

  1. 2026

    Model Collapse Despite Ground-Truth Verification

    Shuo Li Liu

    Hypothesis Testing, ICML 2026

    Workshop poster · Non-archival

    PaperICML programme
    Summary

    Separates exact verification of generated artifacts from coverage of the target task population, showing how missing task types limit risk identification and developing an audit bound for target-risk claims.

    • Related manuscript in preparation: The Lean Anchor Theorem Bounds Shifted Target Risk
  2. 2026

    Bayesian Truth Serum for LLMs?

    Shuo Li Liu

    Statistical Frameworks for Uncertainty in Agentic Systems (AgenticUQ), ICML 2026

    Workshop poster · Non-archival

    PaperICML programmeCode
    Summary

    Tests Bayesian Truth Serum aggregation for language-model panels, finding lower accuracy than majority voting but improved calibration on the evaluated review and benchmark tasks.

    • Companion software: Bayesian Truth-Serum Debate Aggregator
  3. 2026

    Behavior-Only Deployment Certification Requires Bridge Assumptions

    Shuo Li Liu

    Failure Modes in Agentic AI (FAGEN), ICML 2026

    Workshop poster · Non-archival

    PaperICML programme
    Summary

    Shows why behavioral evaluation alone cannot certify deployment safety without assumptions connecting evaluation and deployment histories, and quantifies the sample cost of detecting rarely reached failures.

  4. 2026

    Savage Without Monotonicity

    Shuo Li Liu and Jingni Yang

    Philosophy Meets Machine Learning (PhilML), ICML 2026

    Workshop poster · Non-archival

    PaperICML programme
    Summary

    Studies conditions under which a finitely additive expected-utility representation can tie an act with a statewise improvement, and identifies additional assumptions that prevent this failure of dominance.
